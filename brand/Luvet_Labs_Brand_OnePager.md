## 2. The Name & Story

**Luvet** is short, distinctive, and warm — a made word that sounds real. It carries the soft "love" root without saying it outright: the work we do is careful, considered, and a little bit personal. Every part we ship is one we'd be proud to put in our own home.

**Labs** signals what we actually do — design, iterate, prototype, solve. Luvet Labs is not just a print shop. It is a CAD-first practice that happens to have three printers running.

The brand carries quiet southern hospitality into a market that often feels transactional and cold-quote-driven. Where Stratasys, Protolabs, and Xometry compete on instant quotes and ISO certifications, Luvet Labs competes on conversation, craft, and the weird one-off jobs they will not even bid.

**Origin shorthand:** *New Orleans built. Twin Cities based.*

## 4. Color Palette — "Warm Industrial"

A six-color system. Professional but not corporate, warm but not casual, modern but not trendy.

### Primary Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor | Deep Teal | `#1E5F6B` | 30, 95, 107 | Primary brand color. Logo, headers, primary buttons, anything that needs to read as serious and credible. The 2026 sweet spot — professional like blue, but not invisible like blue. |
| Welcome | Warm Sand | `#E8DFD0` | 232, 223, 208 | Background blocks, story cards, sections that need to feel inviting rather than sterile. Replaces the cold-white SaaS aesthetic with something human. |
| Spark | Burnt Amber | `#D97548` | 217, 117, 72 | Accent for CTAs, link hovers, key statistics, anything that needs to draw the eye. A subtle nod to southern warmth. Use sparingly — accent, not decoration. |

### Supporting Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas | Soft Ivory | `#FAF7F2` | 250, 247, 242 | Default page background. Warmer than pure white — looks photographic, not clinical. |
| Text | Slate Charcoal | `#2C2E33` | 44, 46, 51 | Body copy, footer, dark sections. A zinc-tinted near-black, never pure black — softer, more readable. |
| Quiet | Stone Gray | `#7A7973` | 122, 121, 115 | Secondary text, captions, dividers, metadata. Warm-leaning gray that complements sand and ivory. |

**Palette philosophy:** Most 3D printing services use either sterile tech-blue or aggressive industrial-orange-on-black. Both are crowded and dated. The Warm Industrial system places Luvet Labs in a more sophisticated visual neighborhood — closer to architecture studios, design consultancies, and modern fintechs than to print shops. That visual gap is the brand.

**Palette feels:** Grounded · Modern · Inviting · Crafted · Reliable · Quietly Confident

## 5. Logo & Typography

### The Mark

A monogram — bold "LL" in a soft-cornered square — paired with the wordmark "LUVET · LABS." The interpunct between words signals precision (think laboratory notation) without being aggressive. The mark works alone as an avatar; the full lockup works for headers, business cards, and signage.

### Typography System

| Role | Typeface | Weights | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Manrope | ExtraBold, Bold | Headlines, page titles, hero copy, large numbers |
| Body | Inter | Regular, Medium | Body copy, UI, captions, navigation |

Both fonts available free from Google Fonts. The combination is geometric-but-warm display paired with the most legible neutral body sans available.

### Logo Do's

- Use the monogram alone as a social avatar or favicon
- Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of one "L"
- Use Burnt Amber for the interpunct on light backgrounds
- Reverse to Ivory on Deep Teal for primary applications

### Logo Don'ts

- Do not stretch, skew, or rotate the mark
- Do not place the logo on busy photographs without a solid backdrop
- Do not recolor outside the official palette
- Do not drop the interpunct or use a hyphen instead

### Long (160 words · about page)

Luvet Labs started with a simple observation: the Twin Cities has world-class industrial 3D printing — Stratasys is literally headquartered here — but almost nowhere for a regular person, a small business, or a solo inventor to get one weird part made well, quickly, and at a fair price.

We close that gap. We design in Fusion 360, print on three Bambu P1S machines with full-color AMS capability, and turn most jobs around in three to seven days. Repair parts, prototypes, custom IT and homelab brackets, small-batch product runs, reverse-engineered replacements — if it can be designed and printed, we can probably do it.

The shop is run by an IT professional and CAD designer who grew up in New Orleans and now calls the Twin Cities home. The name carries that story. Every part we make is one we'd be proud to put in our own home.

## 9. Audiences

### Repair Customers

- **What they need:** A specific broken part fixed quickly — appliance knob, drawer clip, vintage stereo dial, washing machine part, snowblower auger piece — without buying a whole new product.
- **How we speak to them:** Plain language. Photos of before and after. "Send me a photo of the broken part. I'll quote within a day."
- **Typical job size:** $40–$200

### Solo Inventors / Etsy Sellers

- **What they need:** 1–3 design iterations of a product idea, without Protolabs' $500 minimums or 2-week lead times.
- **How we speak to them:** Process-led. Show the design steps. Talk about iteration cycles, not just final price.
- **Typical job size:** $200–$1,200

### IT Pros / MSPs / Homelabbers

- **What they need:** Custom rack mounts (1U/2U/half-rack), cable management, AP/switch wall brackets, branded swag for client installs, Pi enclosures, custom KVM/UPS mounts.
- **How we speak to them:** Technical. Use the right vocabulary (1U, half-rack, PoE, Cat6 strain relief). Show you are one of them.
- **Typical job size:** $100–$500 single, $300–$1,500 batch

3. **Domain Captured** — luvetlabs.com secured. Consider adding .co and .dev as defensive backups.
4. **Handle Reservation** — @luvetlabs on Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook, X/Twitter, MakerWorld, Printables, Thingiverse.
